Lesson 10 · about 30 minutes

Capstone: your own extractor, shipped

Everything from Lessons 4–9, with less scaffolding and a bar to clear.

Your win today

You’ll design the training data for a harder extractor, train it, and clear two acceptance bars on a fixed held-out test. Then you’ll publish it on the Hub with an honest model card. That’s the course’s finish line.

The brief

1 · The task: a harder schema

The default schema adds one field: {"name", "age", "city", "email"}, where email is null when the message has none. The prompt says so: “Use null for anything missing.”

The notebook holds a fixed acceptance test of 60 messages. You may read it, but you may not train on it. Its names, cities, email domains and phrasings never appear in your training data, and a check enforces that. It’s harder than Lesson 4’s test in exactly the ways Lesson 8 exposed:

  • 5 of its 8 names and 4 of its 8 cities have two or more words (“Mei Lin”, “Rio de Janeiro”).
  • 26 of the 60 messages have no email.
  • Some messages have other numbers in them: “Has 2 dogs and 3 cats”, “Moved to … in 2019”.
  • The phrasing is messier: lowercase chat, dashes, the email before the name.

Want your own schema instead? Add a field such as phone, then write your own held-out generator by the same rules: unseen values and phrasings, with some rows missing the optional field. The bars stay the same. Your job is the training data. In TRAIN_TEMPLATES you write the phrasings, and you choose the names, cities and domains. The notebook’s fill() turns them into prompt-completion rows, as in Lesson 4.

2 · The loop

design dataLesson 4→ trainLesson 5 or 6→ scoreLesson 8→ read & tag failuresLesson 8↻ shipLesson 9

Start with the fast recipe: Lesson 5’s full SFT of the 135M model. When I ran the finished notebook on a laptop GPU (RTX 3050 Ti), one loop took under 4 minutes, and training took 51 seconds of that. Each time you miss a bar, the notebook prints the failing rows. Tag them as in Lesson 8, change the data, and run the loop again. Switch to LoRA on the 360M model (Lesson 6) only if better data stops helping.

Here’s how it went for me. My first design reused Lesson 4’s phrasings, added an email to half of them, and kept one-word names and cities. It scored 1.00 valid JSON but only 0.85 field accuracy, which misses bar 2. It wrote “Rio” for “Rio de Janeiro” and “Ming” for “Mei Lin”. After I redesigned the data with the Lesson 8 tags in mind, the same recipe reached 1.00 / 0.95. LoRA on the 360M model (r=16) reached 1.00 / 1.00. I won’t show you that design. Building it is the capstone.

The limitations name failures you actually saw. Loading by repo id works in a fresh Colab session: copy the last cell, then paste in your repo id and the prompt.

Do it: the capstone notebook

  1. Download 0010-capstone.ipynb, then in Colab use File → Upload notebook. Choose a T4 GPU, and check that your HF_TOKEN secret is there (Lesson 9).
  2. TODO 1: write at least 8 training phrasings, and extend the name, city and domain lists.
  3. TODO 2: build the trainer from Lesson 5 or 6. Train, then read section 4’s verdict and failures.
  4. Missed a bar? Fix the data in section 2 and rerun from there. Once both bars pass, do TODOs 3 and 4: write your card’s limitations, then push.
  5. Run the last cell. Then open your model page on the Hub and check that the card, the numbers and the “Finetuned from” link are right.

Stuck on the design? The evaluate & ship sheet lists the stress-test checklist and failure tags. The SFT and LoRA sheets hold the training recipes.

Practice

1. On a message with no email, your model replies "email": "kwame@gmail.com". What is the most direct fix?

  1. Add training messages without any email
  2. Train longer on the same rows
  3. Use a tenfold higher learning rate
  4. Switch to the bigger 1.7B model

2. Review (Lesson 9): why reload the saved folder and score it again before pushing?

  1. Proves the shipped files behave identically
  2. Reloading makes the model run faster
  3. The Hub requires a local score
  4. push_to_hub only accepts freshly reloaded models

3. From memory: an interviewer says, “Walk me through your capstone in under a minute.” Answer in four or five sentences.

I fine-tuned SmolLM2 to pull name, age, city and email (or null) out of short messages as JSON. The base model already understood the content but broke the format, so I measured valid-JSON rate and field accuracy with greedy decoding on a held-out test with unseen names, cities and phrasings. My first dataset missed the field-accuracy bar. Error analysis showed it truncated two-word cities and mangled unfamiliar names, so I added those patterns, plus messages without an email, until it cleared 0.95 and 0.90. I merged and re-scored the model, then published it on the Hub with a card covering base model, data, before/after numbers and limitations, and anyone can load it with pipeline.
