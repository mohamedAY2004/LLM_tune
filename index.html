A hands-on course

Fine-tuning chat LLMs with PyTorch & Hugging Face

From Keras transfer learning to a published, fine-tuned text → JSON extractor on the Hugging Face Hub.

What you’ll be able to do

Work as an applied LLM engineer: adapt an open chat model to a product task with SFT and LoRA, and explain every line of the code. The finish line is your own fine-tuned extractor on the Hugging Face Hub, with a before/after evaluation.

Who it’s for: you know neural networks and transfer learning in Keras/TensorFlow, but not PyTorch. Every new PyTorch idea is mapped back to its Keras equivalent. What you need: a free Google Colab or Kaggle account, and a free Hugging Face account for Lessons 9–10.

Each lesson takes 20–30 minutes: a short read, a Colab notebook with self-checking TODOs, and a practice set. Do them in order. Each one builds on the last.

Lessons

Part 1 · The PyTorch underneath

  1. Tokens in, tokens outLoad a chat model, read its chat template, predict and generate tokens, and watch it fail at JSON.
  2. One training step by handWrite the PyTorch training loop yourself and teach the Lesson 1 model exact JSON from one example.
  3. Labels & maskingMask the prompt with -100 so the loss counts only the answer, and see what that changes.

Part 2 · Supervised fine-tuning

  1. A dataset for chatBuild train and held-out test sets in TRL’s prompt-completion format, and see the exact text SFT trains on.
  2. Your first SFT runFully fine-tune SmolLM2-135M with SFTTrainer, and watch the held-out valid-JSON rate jump from about 0 to 1.

Part 3 · Fitting bigger models

  1. LoRAFine-tune a 360M model by training 1.2% of its parameters, and save a 17 MB adapter.
  2. QLoRA on a free GPUDo the memory math, load SmolLM2-1.7B in 4-bit, and fine-tune it on a free T4.

Part 4 · Prove it and ship it

  1. Evaluating an extractorScore base vs tuned on held-out rows, then write stress tests and tag where the model breaks.
  2. Ship itMerge the LoRA adapter, prove nothing changed, and push to the Hub with a model card.
  3. Capstone: your own extractorDesign data for a harder extractor (email or null), clear the acceptance bars, and ship it.
